## The Three Governing Forces

All lateralization behavior emerges from three interacting forces:

**Drift** — the natural tendency of an asymmetric system
to move toward one side when untethered.

**Anchoring** — the stabilizing force that prevents drift
from accumulating into runaway expression.

**Mode** — the postural configuration that determines whether
drift is suppressed, released, or controlled.

These three forces create the full dynamic landscape.

---

## Drift: The Natural Expression of Asymmetry

Drift is what happens when:

- the system is untethered
- amplitude is high
- the postural brace is released
- both hemidiaphragms are free to move independently

Drift is not noise.
It is the architecture expressing its structural asymmetry.

**Sources of drift:**
- Hemidiaphragm imbalance
- Ribcage asymmetry
- Vagal asymmetry
- Baroreceptor timing differences
- Vestibular sensitivity differences
- Visual stream dominance
- Motor dominance
- Spinal rotation bias

**Drift accumulates over time when untethered:**

- Micro-sway →
- Directional lean →
- Rotational bias →
- Full lateralization

This is why long periods in open, relaxed posture often resolve into:
- Leaning to one side
- Rotating in the seat
- Asymmetric gaze
- Asymmetric breath depth

This is not postural failure.
It is unanchored asymmetry expression.

---

## Anchoring: The Counterforce

Anchoring is what turns drift into controlled lateralization.
Without it, drift becomes runaway lateralization.

**Mechanical anchors:**
- One leg stabilizing, bearing more load
- Abdominal brace
- Spinal extension
- Ribcage compression
- Gaze fixation
- Vestibular lock

**Interoceptive anchors:**
- Heartbeat synchronization
- Breath-linked attention
- Gut-pressure awareness

**Environmental anchors:**
- Chair back, armrest, wall
- Object held in one hand
- Floor contact under a dominant foot

Anchoring does not eliminate lateralization.
It gives it a stable end-point.

---

## Mode: The Expression Gate

Mode determines whether lateralization is suppressed,
released, controlled, or runaway.

**Protective Mode**

The brace is engaged. Ribcage is compressed.
Diaphragm is tethered. Vestibular system is stabilized.
Gaze is narrowed.

Result: lateralization is clamped.
Drift is suppressed. The system moves as one unit.

**Exploratory Mode**

The brace releases. Ribcage opens.
Diaphragm is free. Gaze widens.
Vestibular load increases.

Result: lateralization is released.
Drift expresses. Asymmetry becomes visible.

This is why lateralization increases in relaxed, open,
low-pressure conditions — and decreases under urgency,
threat, high cognitive load, or precision demands.

Mode is the gate. Anchoring is the guide. Drift is the current.

## The Drift Cycle

When untethered, lateralization follows a predictable cycle:

1. **Release** — brace drops, asymmetry freed
2. **Drift initiation** — hemidiaphragm imbalance begins to pull
3. **Accumulation** — drift increases with each breath cycle
4. **Threshold** — drift becomes mechanically noticeable
5. **Correction attempt** — micro-sway, gaze shift, or breath
   shift tries to re-center
6. **Reset** — deliberate or automatic reset re-anchors the system
7. **Re-centering** — system returns to neutral

This cycle repeats continuously in exploratory mode.
The rate of cycling is a function of amplitude.

## Reset Mechanisms

The system uses several tools to correct accumulated drift:

**Oculomotor–Vestibular Reset**
Circular eye movements, gaze sweeps, rapid saccades.
Triggered when visual prediction destabilizes from
accumulated asymmetric load.

**Breath Reset**
Deep inhale into the compressed side.
Rebalances hemidiaphragm excursion across the asymmetry.

> **URM grounding:** The breath reset is the `oscillation_operator` recruiting the suppressed hemidiaphragm. Under postural compression, the left hemidiaphragm is mechanically restricted — lower excursion, lower local RSA amplitude, reduced left-sided pulsatility. The breath reset is not a stretch. It is the oscillation amplitude building to the threshold where the restricted side receives sufficient driving pressure to engage.
>
> **Live specimen (2026-08-04):** Left-side recruitment during breath rev-up produces two distinct signals: (1) an audible resistance on left exhale — pressure building behind the postural compression before clearing, and (2) a condensation sensation at the tracheal level — cool air reaching under-ventilated left bronchial pathway, mobilizing static secretions at the carina reunification point. Both signals confirm left hemidiaphragm recruitment has crossed threshold. The rev-up requirement indicates restriction level — amplitude must build on the right side first before the left side engages. Target: left-side recruitment at progressively lower amplitude as postural asymmetry is corrected. Governing primitive: `Mechanical_Coupling`. Governing contract: `contract_GLYMPH_AUTO` — left hemidiaphragm suppression reduces left-sided pulsatility → left-hemisphere glymphatic clearance degraded beyond anatomical baseline asymmetry.

**Sway Reset**
Large sway or deliberate weight shift.
Re-anchors the base of support.

**Spinal Reset**
Unwinding habitual rotation bias.
Re-centers ribcage alignment relative to pelvis.

**Interoceptive Reset**
Heartbeat-synchronized micro-movements.
Re-establishes internal timing anchor.

These are not random behaviors.
They are mechanical corrections — the system managing
its own asymmetry using the tools structurally available to it.

## Amplitude as the Expression Magnifier

Amplitude is the gain on the entire system.
It determines how visible lateralization is,
how fast drift accumulates, and how large corrections need to be.

**Low amplitude:**
- Subtle drift
- Small corrections
- Lateralization barely visible at the surface

**High amplitude:**
- Strong drift
- Large, visible corrections
- Frequent reset cycles

The same architecture, the same asymmetries, the same forces —
expressed at different magnitudes produce dramatically different
observable behavior.

Amplitude does not change the mechanism.
It changes how loud it is.

## Evolutionary Context: Lateralization as Conserved Architecture

The dynamics described in this document are not unique to humans.

Lateralization is present across the entire vertebrate lineage —
fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals all show
consistent population-level lateralization of motor behavior,
sensory preference, and threat response.

This means the architecture predates the human nervous system
by hundreds of millions of years.
It is not a variation. It is not a deviation.
It is the default vertebrate regulatory design.

**The threat/safe split maps directly onto postural mode:**

Under threat, vertebrate systems collapse into bilateral,
symmetric movement — the two sides lock together and
lateralization disappears. This is protective mode.

In safe, exploratory conditions, asymmetric behavior
re-emerges — the two sides decouple and begin expressing
independently. This is exploratory mode.

This pattern appears in fish.

**Eye preference follows the same left/right division:**

Most vertebrates use the right eye for routine tasks and
social interaction, and the left eye for predator scanning
and novelty detection.

Right eye → left hemisphere → precision, routine, detail.
Left eye → right hemisphere → broad sampling, novelty, threat.

This is the visual stream asymmetry already described in
[[Asymmetry Primitives]] — confirmed as ancient architecture,
not human variation.

**Motor dominance follows the same one-anchored pattern:**

Primates, cats, and rats all show individual-level motor
dominance — one side stabilizing while the other moves
freely around it.

The one-anchored lateralization state is not a human quirk.
It is a conserved vertebrate motor strategy.

> **The question is never whether a system lateralizes.
> All vertebrate systems lateralize.
> The question is always what mechanical conditions —
> tethering, amplitude, mode — determine how the
> architecture expresses at any given moment.**

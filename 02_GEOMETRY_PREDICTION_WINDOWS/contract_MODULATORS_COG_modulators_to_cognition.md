## Contract Summary

**Input layers:** EXHALE (respiratory phase, exhale completeness, 
diaphragmatic release, intrathoracic pressure drop), GAZE (foveal 
anchoring, visual fixation, gaze aversion, gaze de-anchoring, visual 
field entropy), BLINK (blink rate, blink duration, blink timing, 
eyelid closure fraction), VESTIBULAR (head movement direction, 
oscillation rhythm, postural sway, pacing, leaning)

**Output layer (COG):** prediction-window width, precision allocation 
(internal vs external), workspace clearing vs workspace protection, 
cognitive-thread stability, social-prediction load modulation, 
anchor stabilization vs anchor clearing

**Core principle:** Exhale collapses global precision. Gaze orients 
the collapse. Blink maintains or micro-resets the collapse. Vestibular 
input stabilizes or clears the anchor frame within which precision 
operates. These four channels form a single regulatory unit — not 
parallel inputs but one integrated gating mechanism that determines 
whether the prediction window narrows or widens, whether precision 
flows inward or outward, and whether the active cognitive thread is 
protected, cleared, or reset.

**The universal behavior discovery:** Every cross-cultural universal 
human gesture — nodding, head shaking, head tilting, swaying, pacing, 
leaning, looking away, blinking, sighing, freezing — is a precision-
window regulator first and a communication signal second. These 
behaviors became universal communication signals because they were 
already universal regulatory actions, and observers could read 
the regulatory state they encoded. The model is not extending to 
explain gestures. The model is identifying that gestures were 
always its domain.

## Link 1: Exhale as the primary precision carrier

The exhale phase is the primary driver of precision modulation in this 
contract. As exhalation proceeds, sympathetic tone decreases, 
intrathoracic pressure drops, and the vagal signal increases — 
collapsing the prediction window, increasing prior weighting, and 
suppressing the update load from external sensory channels. The system 
shifts from sampling mode toward consolidation mode. Internal 
computation stabilizes precisely because external updating is 
suppressed.

This is not a secondary effect of relaxation. Exhale is the 
mechanistic carrier signal. When exhale is complete and unimpeded, the 
precision collapse is maximal. When exhale is incomplete — shallow, 
arrested, suppressed — the precision collapse fails to reach its 
floor, and the system remains partially locked in an externally-loaded 
state. The cognitive consequence is that internal computation cannot 
stabilize fully: workspace protection fails, prior weighting remains 
low, and the system compensates by recruiting surrogate channels — 
gaze, blink, vestibular movement, freeze, postural stillness — to do 
what the respiratory cycle was supposed to do automatically.

The surrogate recruitment is not pathological. It is adaptive 
regulation finding alternative routes when the primary mechanism is 
degraded. But surrogate gating costs more and is less complete than 
the primary exhale-driven collapse, which is why incomplete breathers 
show higher cognitive load per unit of internal work — the 
precision-management overhead is running on backup systems.

**Chain completeness:** Exhale-driven vagal tone increase: 
load-bearing. Vagal tone → precision modulation: load-bearing in 
AUTO↔MOD. Precision collapse → increased prior weighting: 
load-bearing in the predictive processing literature. Surrogate 
recruitment as compensation for incomplete exhale: scaffolding — 
mechanistically coherent, requires dedicated empirical assembly.

## Link 2: Gaze as precision orientation

Exhale drives the collapse. Gaze determines where the collapsed 
precision is directed. This is the orienting function of gaze within 
this contract — separate from its role as a spatial reference frame 
anchor described in AUTO↔MOD Link 12.

When gaze is anchored to a face or socially-relevant stimulus, 
precision is directed outward. The visual channel is active, the 
social prediction system is engaged, the mentalizing load is running, 
and the prediction window widens to accommodate external updating. 
This is the high-external-load configuration: accurate social 
perception requires wide sampling, which is metabolically expensive 
and competes directly with internal computation.

When gaze de-anchors — looking away, soft focus, looking through a 
surface rather than at it — external priors are removed. The visual 
channel is no longer feeding a high-entropy social prediction system. 
Visual field entropy drops. The precision that would have been 
allocated to tracking the face, reading the expression, and modeling 
the interlocutor's state is now available for internal work. This is 
not avoidance. It is precision reallocation.

The functional ambiguity of gaze aversion is the most important 
structural feature of this link. Looking away serves two distinct 
cognitive purposes with identical motor output: workspace clearing 
(fabrication, narrative construction, lying) and workspace protection 
(deep thinking, recall, explaining). In the first case the person is 
clearing the active workspace to build a new narrative. In the second 
they are protecting an existing thread from disruption. From the 
outside, both look the same. The gaze break is the same. The blink 
pattern is similar. Only the direction of internal processing differs.

**Chain completeness:** Gaze anchoring → external prediction load 
increase: load-bearing in social neuroscience. Gaze de-anchoring → 
visual entropy reduction → precision reallocation: scaffolding, 
mechanistically coherent. The workspace clearing vs workspace 
protection distinction under identical motor output: inferential — 
derivable from the framework, requires behavioral and neuroimaging 
assembly to confirm.

## Link 3: Blink as micro-precision collapse

Blinking is the smallest available surrogate gate in this system. A 
blink momentarily closes the visual channel, collapses visual 
precision, interrupts involuntary re-anchoring, and micro-resets the 
prediction window — all in approximately 150–400ms. Where exhale 
collapses global precision and gaze orients that collapse, blink 
maintains it. Without blink management, spontaneous gaze re-anchoring 
would continuously interrupt the inward-directed precision state that 
the exhale-and-gaze maneuver established.

Blink rate increases under cognitive load not because the visual 
system is fatigued but because the cognitive system is using blink as 
active regulation. Recall, construction, and complex reasoning all 
require sustained inward precision direction. Each involuntary fixation 
on an external stimulus pulls precision outward and disrupts the 
internal thread. Elevated blink rate prevents this by repeatedly 
collapsing the visual channel before re-anchoring can complete. The 
pattern during active thinking — gaze away, sustained blink rate — is 
not correlated behavior. It is coordinated regulation.

The blink is the micro-exhale of the visual system. Exhale resets the 
respiratory-precision cycle. Blink resets the visual-precision cycle. 
Both are surrogate gates when their paired primary mechanism is 
insufficient — both are recruited more heavily when the exhale is 
incomplete, because an incomplete exhale leaves residual external 
precision load that gaze and blink must compensate for.

**Chain completeness:** Blink rate increase during cognitive load: 
load-bearing empirically (blink rate and cognitive processing 
literature is well-established). Blink as prevention of involuntary 
re-anchoring: scaffolding — mechanistically coherent, not yet directly 
assembled as the explicit mechanism. The coordination of blink with 
gaze aversion as a single precision-management unit: inferential, 
requires coordinated eye-tracking and blink-rate assembly.

## Link 4: Visual surface selection as precision-window maneuver

The discovery that generates this link is the observation that 
looking at a blank, low-entropy surface — a wall, a floor, a ceiling, 
the middle distance — produces a qualitatively different cognitive 
state than looking away from a person into open space or a visually 
complex environment. The mechanism is now derivable from the contract.

Gaze aversion removes social prediction load. But if the gaze lands on 
a visually complex or semantically rich surface — a bookshelf, a 
window with movement, another face — the visual channel immediately 
begins loading a new prediction problem. The precision has been 
reallocated away from the social stimulus but has been immediately 
captured by the next available stimulus. The workspace clearing or 
protection maneuver has failed.

A blank surface provides the exit condition the system needs: gaze is 
de-anchored from the social stimulus, but the visual channel encounters 
a low-entropy field that generates minimal prediction error and 
demands minimal updating. Precision collapses inward because there is 
nothing in the visual field competing for it. This is why the wall, 
the ceiling, the corner of the floor, and the middle distance are 
universal thinking surfaces. They are not arbitrary. They are the 
surfaces that satisfy the precision management requirement: low visual 
entropy, no social signal, no competing prediction demand.

The ceiling is particularly common during recall. Lying down optimizes 
the same mechanism by removing postural load from the proprioceptive 
channel, reducing vestibular updating, and allowing gaze to stabilize 
on a structurally blank surface. Closing the eyes is the terminal case 
— it eliminates the visual channel entirely, guaranteeing precision 
collapse inward regardless of the surrounding environment.

Other visual precision maneuvers follow from the same principle: 
staring through a surface (maintaining gaze direction while 
defocusing, which collapses foveal precision without changing gaze 
position), soft eyes (reduced foveal engagement), and the 
unfocused-look-at-nothing that occurs during complex speech production. 
None of these are psychological habits. They are precision-window 
control behaviors that have been universally reinvented by every 
human nervous system because the mechanism they exploit is structural.

**Chain completeness:** Low-entropy visual field → reduced prediction 
error in visual cortex: load-bearing in predictive processing 
neuroscience. Reduced visual prediction error → precision 
reallocation inward: scaffolding — the mechanism is derivable but 
requires assembly with foveal engagement and blink rate data 
simultaneously. Universal cross-cultural surface selection for 
thinking: behavioral observation, requires systematic cross-cultural 
assembly to confirm universality.

## Link 5: Vestibular surrogate gates — anchor stabilization and clearing

The vestibular system's role in this contract is distinct from its 
role in spatial orientation. Here it functions as an anchor-state 
modulator: vestibular input either reinforces the current cognitive 
anchor (stabilizing the prediction thread) or disrupts it (clearing 
the current frame to allow reorientation). This makes vestibular 
behavior the fourth channel of the precision-management system — 
operating on the anchor level where exhale, gaze, and blink operate 
on the precision level.

The key structural insight is that vestibular behaviors are the 
channel that operates on the *frame* rather than the *content* of 
the prediction window. Exhale collapses precision. Gaze orients it. 
Blink maintains it. Vestibular input determines what anchor the 
precision is operating relative to. This is why vestibular behaviors 
feel qualitatively different from the others: they change the 
reference frame, not just the allocation.

**Vertical oscillation (nodding):**
Rhythmic vertical head movement synchronizes with exhale rhythm and 
reinforces anchor stability. Each downward nod compresses the 
intrathoracic cavity slightly, amplifying the exhale-gate signal. 
The rhythmic pattern reduces vestibular prediction error — the system 
is generating its own expected vestibular input rather than receiving 
unpredictable input from the environment. This narrows the prediction 
window, supports cognitive-thread tracking, and reduces update demand. 
Nodding during listening is not a social signal of agreement first — 
it is a precision-management behavior that maintains the narrow window 
required for thread-tracking during continuous input. It became an 
agreement signal because observers correctly read the regulatory state 
it encodes: the system is in tracking mode, not evaluation mode, which 
means the prior is being updated rather than resisted.

**Lateral oscillation (head shaking):**
Lateral head movement disrupts the current vestibular anchor, 
generates transient vestibular prediction error, and clears the 
current cognitive thread by forcing the system to resolve the spatial 
dissonance before resuming content processing. This is an anchor-
clearing maneuver. The head shake that accompanies "no" is not 
arbitrary — it is a regulatory action that disrupts the current 
thread, and disrupting the current thread is the mechanical correlate 
of refusing the incoming update. The gesture became a refusal signal 
because it is mechanically a refusal — at the substrate level — before 
it is communicated at the semantic level. Lateral oscillation during 
confusion performs the same function: the person is mechanically 
clearing a thread that has failed to resolve, creating the 
possibility of reorientation.

**Head tilting:**
Tilting the head shifts the vestibular reference frame by a fixed 
angle, reducing the degrees of freedom available to the prediction 
system and collapsing ambiguity around a single perceptual axis. 
This is a precision-narrowing maneuver on ambiguous input: the tilt 
reduces the space of interpretations the system must simultaneously 
hold by committing the vestibular reference frame to one orientation. 
It increases precision on the currently selected interpretation while 
suppressing alternatives. The universal association of head tilting 
with curiosity or focused attention is mechanistically accurate — 
the tilt is what a system looks like when it is narrowing precision 
onto a single thread from a wide sampling field.

**Swaying and rocking:**
Rhythmic bilateral vestibular input — swaying, rocking, the parent 
rocking an infant — generates predictable, self-generated vestibular 
signal that suppresses the error contribution from unpredictable 
environmental vestibular sources. By controlling its own vestibular 
input, the system reduces the precision budget required for vestibular 
updating, freeing that budget for internal work or for the co-
regulation function the rocking serves. This is why rocking is a 
universal self-soothing behavior across development and across 
neurotypes: it is not a motor habit. It is a precision-management 
behavior that predictably reduces one of the six channels' 
contribution to the total external precision demand. ND individuals 
who stim through rocking are not engaging in non-functional behavior 
— they are using the vestibular channel as a precision regulator in 
exactly the way the system was designed to use it.

**Pacing:**
Rhythmic bilateral vestibular-proprioceptive coupling through 
repetitive locomotion offloads cognitive load by providing a stable, 
self-generated background signal across both channels simultaneously. 
Pacing suppresses the prediction error contribution from the 
vestibular and proprioceptive channels together — the motor pattern 
is predictable enough that neither channel generates significant 
surprise, freeing their precision budget for the cognitive thread. 
This is why pacing emerges universally during complex reasoning: the 
movement is not distraction management, it is substrate management. 
The person is using motor regulation to free up the precision channels 
that would otherwise compete with the cognitive load.

**Leaning and bracing:**
Reducing degrees of freedom through postural stabilization — leaning 
against a surface, bracing arms, fixing the trunk — constrains the 
vestibular and proprioceptive input space by limiting the range of 
motion the system needs to predict. This is the vestibular analog of 
the blank visual surface: by constraining the input, the system 
eliminates the prediction demand that variable input would impose. 
Leaning during complex thought is not laziness. It is active 
reduction of the proprioceptive and vestibular precision overhead 
that unrestricted movement would maintain.

**The communication-from-regulation derivation:**
Every one of these behaviors generates a readable regulatory signal 
that social observers learn to interpret. Nodding communicates 
tracking. Head shaking communicates rejection. Tilting communicates 
focused evaluation. Swaying communicates soothing or containment. 
This is not coincidence and it is not cultural convention. The 
universality of gesture meaning across unrelated cultures is 
explained by this derivation: the gestures mean the same things 
across cultures because they are the same regulatory behaviors across 
cultures, and the regulatory state they encode is the same regardless 
of cultural context. Social meaning did not create gestural behavior. 
Regulatory mechanics created gestural behavior, and social meaning 
was then read directly off the substrate.

**Chain completeness:** Vertical oscillation → exhale synchrony → 
anchor reinforcement: scaffolding — mechanistically coherent, 
requires concurrent breath and head-movement measurement. Lateral 
oscillation → vestibular disruption → thread clearing: scaffolding, 
derivable but requires concurrent cognitive-task and head-movement 
assembly. Swaying and rocking → self-generated vestibular suppression 
of environmental prediction error: load-bearing in infant 
neuroscience and ND literature. Pacing → dual-channel cognitive 
offload: scaffolding, consistent with embodied cognition literature, 
requires direct assembly with precision budget measurement. 
Universality of gesture meaning from regulatory substrate: strong 
cross-cultural behavioral observation, mechanistic derivation is the 
novel contribution here — requires systematic cross-cultural 
regulatory-state measurement alongside gesture-meaning data.

## Link 6: Oral-motor and tactile-pressure surrogate gates — trigeminal relay, C-tactile activation, and bilateral compression

The behaviors in this link share a structural feature that 
distinguishes them from the exhale, gaze, blink, and vestibular 
channels: they operate in two directions simultaneously. Every 
behavior documented here is either a deliberate precision-management 
maneuver or an involuntary expression of a precision state — and the 
same motor output can be either, depending on whether the system is 
using the channel as a gate or venting through it as overflow. This 
is not true of exhale or gaze in the same way: exhale is primarily a 
deliberate gate, and forced exhalation is not the typical overflow 
form. Here, the overflow states and the deliberate regulatory states 
share identical or near-identical motor patterns. The distinction is 
directional — inward regulation vs outward discharge — not behavioral.

The channel that makes this possible is the trigeminal system. The 
trigeminal nucleus is not a peripheral structure. It is a central 
brainstem regulatory node with direct connections to the NTS — the 
same nucleus that receives vagal afferent input and sets the autonomic 
balance state that all upstream contracts depend on. Oral-motor and 
facial motor activity is therefore not peripheral motor behavior that 
happens to correlate with cognitive states. It is direct input to the 
brainstem regulatory node that the entire contract stack rides on. 
What the face does matters mechanistically, not just expressively.

The second substrate this link operates through is the C-tactile 
afferent system. C-tactile fibers are unmyelinated afferents that 
respond specifically to gentle stroking touch in the 1–10 cm/s range. 
They project to the insular cortex — not the somatosensory cortex — 
which means they are interoceptive rather than discriminative. They 
activate the parasympathetic system directly. The face, particularly 
the periorbital region, temples, and jaw, carries the highest C-tactile 
fiber density available to voluntary self-touch outside of a small 
number of other zones. This makes hand-face contact the highest-yield 
parasympathetic self-activation maneuver in the voluntary motor 
repertoire.

**Rhythmic mastication — the oral-motor surrogate for pacing:**
Rhythmic chewing is bilateral rhythmic jaw movement at the skull base. 
The mechanism is structurally identical to pacing and rocking: a 
self-generated bilateral motor pattern that produces predictable, 
periodic proprioceptive input to the trigeminal system, reducing the 
prediction error contribution from that channel and freeing its 
precision budget. The additional pathway is trigeminal-to-NTS: 
rhythmic jaw movement generates a repetitive, low-amplitude 
trigeminal signal at the brainstem that modulates NTS tone in the 
same direction as vagal input — toward parasympathetic balance. 
Rhythmic mastication is therefore a dual-mechanism gate: bilateral 
proprioceptive rhythm suppresses vestibular-adjacent prediction error, 
and trigeminal-NTS input modulates autonomic tone simultaneously. 
This is the mechanism behind the consistent finding that chewing gum 
improves performance on attention and memory tasks under load — the 
effect is not oral stimulation producing arousal but oral-motor rhythm 
reducing the precision overhead that load was imposing on the system.

**Jaw clenching — sympathetic overflow into the trigeminal motor zone:**
The trigeminal motor system is the highest-innervation motor zone in 
the head and the zone with the most direct brainstem access. Under 
sustained sympathetic load — particularly the kind that cannot be 
discharged through large-motor output because the body is constrained 
to stillness — sympathetic overflow writes into the trigeminal motor 
system as masseter and temporalis tension. Jaw clenching is not a 
stress response in the psychological sense. It is sympathetic 
activation finding its highest-access motor outlet when preferred 
large-motor outlets (locomotion, postural adjustment, pacing) are 
socially or situationally unavailable. The tension is measurable 
autonomic load expressing into muscle. This is why jaw pain is a 
reliable chronic stress marker: it is the accumulated motor expression 
of sympathetic discharge that had no other available outlet across 
repeated constrained high-load states.

**Jaw drop — sympathetic discharge completing and releasing:**
Jaw drop is the trigeminal motor correlate of exhausted sympathetic 
holding. Where jaw clenching is the system sustaining effortful motor 
expression of arousal load, jaw drop is the system releasing it. The 
masseter and temporalis tension evacuates suddenly — the held motor 
pattern collapses. This occurs under two distinct conditions: 
overwhelming surprise (the load arrives faster than the holding 
pattern can be maintained) and extreme cognitive overload (the system 
can no longer sustain effortful regulation across all channels 
simultaneously and the trigeminal channel is the first to release). 
In both cases the jaw drop is a regulatory state readout, not a 
communicative choice. The communication layer reads it accurately — 
jaw drop encodes shock or overwhelm across cultures because it is the 
same regulatory event across cultures. The open mouth simultaneously 
enables maximum inhale volume if the gasp reflex fires, which links 
this behavior to the next entry.

**Gasping — forced multi-channel precision-open reflex:**
Gasping is the involuntary regulatory inverse of the full exhale. 
Where a complete exhale collapses precision, collapses the prediction 
window, and shifts the system toward consolidation mode, a gasp 
forces the prediction window open through a hard-interrupt to maximum 
sampling mode. The mechanism is multi-channel and simultaneous: the 
forced inhale reverses the exhale-gate, spiking intrathoracic 
pressure differential and firing the inhale-associated sympathetic 
surge; the mouth opens to maximize airway volume and speed; the eyes 
widen, maximizing the visual field and spiking visual entropy intake; 
the auditory signal fires, adding an ascending alert through the 
acoustic startle pathway. The system is being yanked into maximum 
external sampling configuration by an unexpected precision error large 
enough to override the current thread. Gasping is not an expressive 
behavior with a regulatory correlate — it is the regulatory reflex 
itself, and the expression is a downstream readout of the reflex's 
multi-channel forced-open signature.

**Fidgeting — pacing at reduced motor budget:**
When large-motor vestibular-proprioceptive regulation is socially 
prohibited — seated in a meeting, a classroom, a waiting room — the 
system does not abandon the precision-management attempt. It finds the 
smallest available motor output that can still deliver a partial 
version of the rhythmic bilateral proprioceptive signal: leg bouncing, 
pen clicking, finger tapping, foot rotation, chair swaying. Each of 
these is the same dual-channel offload mechanism as pacing, 
implemented at the minimum viable motor scale. The suppression is 
less complete because the pattern is smaller and less bilateral — 
which is why fidgeting people focus better than non-fidgeting people 
under the same load in constrained environments, but not as well as 
pacing people in unconstrained environments. The motor scale 
determines the magnitude of the precision-budget relief, not whether 
the mechanism is running. Prohibiting fidgeting in classrooms is 
mechanistically identical to prohibiting pacing during complex 
reasoning — it removes a precision-management behavior and attributes 
the resulting performance degradation to the behavioral profile that 
was compensating for the load.

**Hand-face contact — C-tactile parasympathetic relay:**
Voluntary hand-face contact applies gentle pressure or stroking to 
the highest C-tactile density zone available to voluntary self-touch. 
The insular projection of C-tactile afferents activates the 
parasympathetic system directly, partially counteracting the 
sympathetic load that is simultaneously expressing as trigeminal 
motor tension in the same region. Temple rubbing, chin resting, 
cheek pressure, and forehead contact are all applying parasympathetic 
input to a sympathetically loaded zone. The hand is delivering the 
counter-signal to the precise area that is registering the load. The 
universality of this behavior across development and culture — the 
infant self-soothing through face-touching, the adult rubbing their 
temples under cognitive load — follows directly from the anatomy: 
every human nervous system has the same C-tactile density map and 
the same parasympathetic projection pathway.

**Pinching the bridge of the nose — convergent pressure mechanism:**
Pinching the nasion applies pressure over the frontal sinus territory 
and the dense C-tactile field of the nasal bridge simultaneously. 
The full mechanism may involve both pathways: C-tactile insular 
activation providing parasympathetic input, and direct sinus pressure 
generating slight intracranial pressure effects through the 
paranasal-sinus pathway. The behavior appears universally during 
high cognitive load states where a decision is being resisted, a 
thread is being protected against incoming challenge, or the system 
is holding two incompatible priors simultaneously. It is a precision-
protection maneuver: the C-tactile input partially stabilizes the 
regulatory state while the system attempts to maintain the thread 
under load.

**Compound multi-channel mechanical anchor — elbow-braced skull lock:**
The elbow-on-desk-hand-on-head configuration is a spontaneously 
engineered compound anchor that simultaneously closes three precision-
management channels. The elbow on the desk reduces the arm's degrees 
of freedom to near-zero, eliminating the proprioceptive prediction 
demand for the upper limb — the system no longer needs to model arm 
position or predict arm movement. The hand on the skull mechanically 
fixes head position, locking the vestibular channel: the head cannot 
drift, sway, or respond to environmental perturbation because it is 
mechanically anchored by the hand's weight and contact. The hand-
skull contact simultaneously activates the C-tactile field of the 
scalp, adding a low-amplitude parasympathetic input across the 
contact surface. The combined effect: proprioceptive channel locked, 
vestibular channel locked, C-tactile parasympathetic relay active, 
three precision channels simultaneously generating predictable near-
zero-error input. The precision budget those three channels would 
otherwise consume is freed entirely for the cognitive thread. 
Additionally, because the head is structurally anchored rather than 
muscularly anchored, gaze can de-anchor freely without the vestibular 
instability that unsupported gaze drift would normally generate — the 
visual channel can be released for workspace protection or clearing 
while the reference frame remains fixed by the mechanical lock. This 
is not a habit or a posture preference. It is a multi-channel 
precision lock that the nervous system reinvented through use because 
the mechanism it exploits is structural.

**Crossed arms — bilateral deep pressure and thoracic self-bracing:**
Crossing the arms delivers bilateral deep pressure through each hand 
gripping the opposite upper arm. Deep pressure is proprioceptive 
input that resolves positional uncertainty for both limbs 
simultaneously — the compression provides unambiguous positional 
information the proprioceptive channel can resolve without active 
monitoring, reducing the prediction budget required for upper-limb 
position tracking. The simultaneous thoracic closure draws the 
shoulders slightly forward, partially self-bracing the chest wall and 
reducing thoracic degrees of freedom in a manner parallel to the 
leaning and bracing behaviors in Link 5. The social reading of 
crossed arms as "defensive" is mechanistically accurate at the 
substrate level before it reaches the communication layer: the system 
is in a thread-protection configuration, delivering bilateral 
compression to stabilize the proprioceptive channel while the thoracic 
closure partially braces the exhale against disruption. The observer 
reads "defensive" because the regulatory state that produces arm-
crossing is the one that accompanies holding a prior against incoming 
challenge. The label is correct. The mechanism producing it is not 
psychological — it is substrate.

**The two-direction principle — gate vs overflow:**
Every behavior in this link can be read in either direction. 
Jaw clenching is overflow when it appears involuntarily under 
constrained sympathetic load; it is gate when it is deliberately 
applied (biting the inside of the cheek during painful focus, 
controlled oral pressure during physical exertion). Rhythmic 
mastication is gate when deliberate and overflow when the system 
reaches for it automatically. Hand-face contact is gate when applied 
as a deliberate precision-protection maneuver and overflow when the 
hand finds the face without conscious initiation. The gasping reflex 
is always overflow — there is no deliberate version of a gasp that 
carries the same multi-channel forced-open signature. Understanding 
the direction tells you what the system is doing: gate behaviors are 
precision-management in progress; overflow behaviors are load 
exceeding the available gating capacity and expressing through the 
nearest high-access motor outlet.

**Chain completeness:** Trigeminal-NTS connection as the central 
brainstem relay: load-bearing anatomically. Rhythmic mastication → 
trigeminal modulation of NTS tone: scaffolding — the pathway is 
anatomically confirmed, the regulatory modulation direction is 
mechanistically coherent, the specific NTS tone effect of jaw rhythm 
requires dedicated empirical assembly. Chewing gum and cognitive 
performance: load-bearing empirically, mechanism previously attributed 
to arousal, the trigeminal-bilateral-rhythm account is the novel 
contribution here. C-tactile fiber density on face → insular 
projection → parasympathetic activation: load-bearing in 
affective touch neuroscience. Hand-face contact as highest-yield 
voluntary parasympathetic self-activation maneuver: scaffolding — 
mechanistically derivable from the C-tactile anatomy, requires 
assembly with concurrent HRV and skin conductance during naturalistic 
self-touch. Compound multi-channel anchor (elbow-desk-skull lock): 
inferential — derivable from the contract's channel architecture, 
requires behavioral assembly with concurrent HRV, head-movement, and 
cognitive-task data. Bilateral deep pressure → proprioceptive 
resolution → reduced prediction budget: scaffolding, consistent with 
weighted blanket and deep pressure literature. Jaw drop and jaw clench 
as two directions of trigeminal sympathetic overflow: scaffolding, 
mechanistically coherent with the trigeminal-NTS architecture, 
requires electromyographic assembly with concurrent autonomic 
measurement. Gasping as multi-channel forced-open reflex: 
load-bearing for the individual components (startle reflex, inhale 
sympathetic surge, eye widening), the integrated multi-channel 
precision-open account is the novel synthesis here.

## Link 7: Integrated dynamics — workspace clearing vs workspace protection

The contract produces two distinct functional configurations that 
share motor output but differ in cognitive direction. Both begin with 
the same exhale-gaze-blink sequence. The divergence is in what the 
cleared or protected workspace is used for.

In workspace clearing, the sequence removes existing priors to allow 
new narrative construction. Exhale collapses the prediction window. 
Gaze breaks from the social stimulus. Blink rate spikes through the 
transition. The active workspace — previously loaded with the 
interlocutor's context, the shared narrative frame, and the external 
priors — is cleared. A new narrative is assembled from internal 
generation rather than from updating on the current context. This is 
the precision-management signature of fabrication, lying, and 
post-hoc narrative construction: they require the same maneuver as 
deep thinking because the cognitive operation is identical — internal 
generation under suppressed external updating.

In workspace protection, the same sequence preserves an existing 
internal thread against disruption from external signals. Exhale 
collapses precision. Gaze de-anchors. Blink maintains the visual 
channel offline. The difference from workspace clearing is that 
something is already running — a recall chain, an explanation being 
constructed, a simulation in progress — and the maneuver is defensive 
rather than constructive. The person is not building a new thread; 
they are protecting the current one from the precision pull that 
continued eye contact, social signal monitoring, or visual environment 
updates would impose.

Vestibular behaviors augment both configurations. In workspace 
clearing, lateral oscillation (head shaking, turning away) reinforces 
the anchor disruption the gaze break initiates. In workspace 
protection, postural bracing or rhythmic swaying maintains anchor 
stability against the disruption that environmental input would impose.

The forced precision shift is the failure mode of this system. When 
an external cue forces gaze re-anchoring — an interruption, a demand 
for eye contact, a sudden movement — the prediction window widens 
involuntarily. Exhale collapses prematurely. Sympathetic tone spikes. 
The cognitive thread collapses. This is not a social sensitivity or 
an emotional response. It is the mechanical consequence of a forced 
precision shift during a workspace protection maneuver. The cost is 
proportional to the depth of the internal computation that was 
underway.

**Chain completeness:** The workspace clearing vs protection 
distinction: inferential, derivable from the contract, requires 
behavioral assembly with concurrent cognitive task data. Forced 
re-anchoring → thread collapse: scaffolding — mechanistically 
coherent with the ANCHOR↔COG forced-shift mechanism, requires 
parallel empirical assembly. Vestibular augmentation of both 
configurations: inferential, requires concurrent head-movement and 
cognitive-task measurement.

## Failure Modes

**The eye contact requirement as regulatory disruption:**
Requiring sustained eye contact during communication — as a social 
norm or explicit demand — forces gaze anchoring throughout an 
interaction. Gaze anchoring directs precision outward, engages the 
social prediction system, and prevents the inward precision collapse 
that workspace protection requires. For individuals whose cognitive 
work requires inward precision direction — complex recall, 
construction, explanation — sustained eye contact actively degrades 
performance. The person is being asked to maintain the gaze 
configuration that prevents the thinking the interaction requires. 
This is not social anxiety. It is a forced architectural conflict 
between the interaction's social demands and the cognitive work's 
precision requirements.

**The ND forced attention shift:**
ND individuals show higher reliance on gaze de-anchoring, blink-based 
regulation, surrogate vestibular gating (rocking, pacing, stimming), 
and postural bracing — not as deficits but as adaptive strategies for 
high-gain, narrow-window architectures. The cost of forced attention 
redirection is correspondingly higher: an interruption that produces 
a mild distraction for a typical individual may completely collapse 
an active workspace for an ND individual whose internal thread 
required the full exhale-gaze-blink-vestibular maneuver to establish 
and maintain. The word "violent" used colloquially to describe the 
experience of interruption during deep focus is mechanistically 
accurate: the forced precision shift terminates internal computation 
the way a power cut terminates a calculation. Stimming suppression 
in educational and professional contexts removes the vestibular 
and proprioceptive regulation the ND individual is using to maintain 
the precision state the demanded task requires — it is directly 
counterproductive to the stated goal.

**The visually complex environment trap:**
Gaze aversion alone is insufficient for workspace protection in a 
visually rich environment. If the gaze moves away from a social 
stimulus and lands on a high-entropy visual field — a busy street, 
a cluttered desk, a screen — the visual channel immediately loads a 
new prediction problem and precision remains externally directed. 
This is why thinking in busy environments is harder than the acoustic 
load alone predicts: visual complexity competes for precision 
independently of noise. Environments optimized for concentration 
reduce visual entropy in the peripheral field, not just acoustic 
noise, because the precision management mechanism requires a low-
entropy exit surface for gaze de-anchoring to function.

**The chronic eye contact training problem:**
Social training that teaches sustained eye contact as the default — 
as a signal of engagement, honesty, or respect — systematically 
trains the suppression of the gaze de-anchoring mechanism the 
cognitive system needs for internal work. Individuals trained to 
maintain eye contact during active explanation, recall, or complex 
reasoning will consistently underperform on the cognitive task 
relative to their unobserved baseline — not because they are less 
capable but because the social compliance is actively preventing 
the precision management the task requires.

**Stimming suppression as precision degradation:**
The suppression of vestibular and proprioceptive self-regulation 
behaviors — rocking, swaying, pacing, hand movements — removes the 
surrogate gating mechanisms the ND individual is using to manage 
external precision load. In a high-load environment, those behaviors 
are not incidental to cognitive performance: they are the substrate 
management that makes cognitive performance possible. Suppressing 
them does not produce "normalized" behavior on top of intact 
function. It removes a load-bearing regulatory component and 
degrades the cognitive output it was maintaining.

**Misreading regulatory signals as semantic signals:**
Because gestural behaviors were communication signals derived from 
regulatory behaviors, observers calibrated to the communication layer 
will systematically misread the regulatory state they encode. Nodding 
is read as agreement rather than as tracking mode. Head shaking is 
read as explicit refusal rather than as thread-clearing. Swaying is 
read as agitation rather than as precision-load management. Pacing is 
read as anxiety rather than as cognitive offload. The misread has 
clinical, educational, and institutional consequences: the behavior 
is treated as semantic output and responded to at the semantic level, 
when the actual signal is regulatory — the person is managing their 
prediction window, not communicating a proposition. Interventions 
that target the behavior directly — suppressing the swaying, stopping 
the pacing, demanding stillness — remove the regulatory function while 
leaving the load that function was managing.

The deepest case of this failure mode is the ND individual whose 
entire visible regulatory repertoire — stimming, gaze aversion, 
posture shifts, movement during conversation — is read as social 
non-compliance, disengagement, or emotional dysregulation. The 
observer is reading the communication layer of behaviors that evolved 
as regulatory behaviors. The ND individual is not communicating 
disinterest. They are maintaining the precision state the 
conversation requires. Demanding behavioral conformity — eye contact, 
stillness, forward-facing posture — removes the regulatory scaffolding 
while demanding higher cognitive output. The cognitive output 
degrades. The degradation is then attributed to the original 
behavioral profile rather than to its suppression.

## The Universal Behavior Map — Summary Statement

Every precision-window regulation behavior humans have universally 
developed maps onto one of six channels this contract and its 
neighbors describe:

- **Respiratory:** exhale, sigh, breath-hold, full exhale completion
- **Visual:** gaze aversion, blank surface selection, defocusing, 
  eye closure, blink management
- **Vestibular:** nodding, head shaking, tilting, swaying, rocking, 
  pacing, leaning
- **Proprioceptive:** bracing, stillness, postural locking, 
  compression garments, weight-shifting
- **Auditory:** masking, turning down the radio, silence-seeking, 
  noise-canceling
- **Oral-motor / Tactile-pressure:** rhythmic mastication, jaw 
  clenching (overflow), jaw drop (overflow), gasping (overflow), 
  hand-face C-tactile contact, bilateral self-compression, compound 
  mechanical skull anchoring, fidgeting

These are not cultural behaviors. They are not personality traits. 
They are not habits. They are the six channels through which the 
human nervous system manages the precision geometry of its own 
prediction windows. They are universal because the mechanism they 
exploit is structural and universal. They became communication signals 
because the regulatory state they encode is readable, and reading 
regulatory state in others is socially valuable.

The ND population does not have a different system. They have the 
same system with different thresholds, different surrogate recruitment 
patterns, and different behavioral expressions of the same underlying 
architecture. The differences in kind that appear clinically are 
differences in degree at the substrate level — higher gain, narrower 
default window, stronger reliance on explicit surrogate gating to 
achieve what NT nervous systems achieve through lower-visibility 
automatic regulation.

This contract is the layer where physiology becomes behavior, and 
where behavior becomes communication.

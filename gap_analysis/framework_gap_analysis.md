# Regulatory Framework — Gap Analysis
## Where the literature likely exists but hasn't been assembled

---

## Method

Each gap is categorized by:
- **Type A** — Literature almost certainly exists, just not assembled across the silo boundary
- **Type B** — Data exists as secondary outcomes in other research, not the primary question
- **Type C** — Requires new research, no obvious existing dataset

Priority is highest where Type A and B gaps confirm load-bearing chains.

---

## AUTO↔MOD Gaps

### Gap 1: Hemidiaphragm asymmetry as involuntary lateralization
**The gap:** Deliberate postural asymmetry producing hemispheric lateralization shift hasn't been studied. But involuntary asymmetry has — scoliosis produces exactly the thoracic compression the contract describes, unintentionally.

**Likely literature:**
- Scoliosis and autonomic function — researchers measuring HRV in scoliosis patients are likely finding asymmetric autonomic patterns without knowing why
- Hemidiaphragm paralysis studies — unilateral phrenic nerve damage produces forced hemidiaphragm asymmetry; any cognitive or autonomic sequelae confirms the mechanism from a clinical angle
- Unilateral nasal breathing research — breathing preferentially through one nostril is documented to shift hemispheric dominance; the hemidiaphragm asymmetry may be part of the mechanism

**What assembly would confirm:** That involuntary thoracic asymmetry produces measurable lateralization bias in the predicted direction — establishing the mechanism before the deliberate training version is studied.

**Type:** A

---

### Gap 2: EMDR as oculomotor-vagal mechanism
**The gap:** The gaze stabilization → oculomotor-vagal → NTS pathway is labeled adjacent. But EMDR — bilateral eye movement during trauma processing — has an extensive clinical literature. EMDR researchers are studying trauma outcomes, not the autonomic mechanism producing them.

**Likely literature:**
- EMDR and HRV — some studies measure autonomic state during EMDR; the bilateral eye movement component should produce specific vagal tone changes if the oculomotor-NTS pathway is real
- Eye movement desensitization component studies — research separating eye movement from other EMDR components; any component showing HRV change isolates the oculomotor-vagal pathway
- Smooth pursuit eye movement and autonomic tone — basic oculomotor research may have measured HR or HRV as incidental outcomes

**What assembly would confirm:** That the therapeutic effect of EMDR's eye movement component is at least partially autonomic — oculomotor input to NTS producing vagal tone increase that enables the precision weighting collapse required for prior updating. This would move the gaze-vagal link from adjacent to scaffolding.

**Type:** A/B

---

### Gap 3: Freediving literature as kumbhaka research
**The gap:** Kumbhaka in trained practitioners hasn't been studied because researchers use untrained populations. Freedivers develop extraordinary CO2 tolerance through exactly the training mechanism the contract describes.

**Likely literature:**
- Freediving and cognitive performance during breath holds — safety research measuring cognitive function at depth; the CO2 tolerance mechanism is documented without being named as regulatory
- Static apnea and autonomic state — competitive breath-hold diving has physiological monitoring; HRV signatures during sustained holds are available
- Altitude acclimatization and chemoreceptor recalibration — hypoxic training produces central chemoreceptor adaptation; confirms the recalibration principle

**What assembly would confirm:** That trained breath-hold populations show cognitive performance profiles during holds distinct from naive populations — consistent with suspended carrier state producing a different cognitive mode rather than hypoxic impairment.

**Type:** B

---

### Gap 4: Vestibular stimulation and autonomic function
**The gap:** Vestibular-NTS projections are anatomically confirmed. The deliberate regulatory use is unstudied. But vestibular stimulation research exists in motion sickness, space medicine, and occupational therapy.

**Likely literature:**
- Galvanic vestibular stimulation (GVS) and HRV — GVS is used in research; autonomic effects are measured as safety outcomes but likely show the vagal tone modulation the contract predicts
- Sensory integration therapy outcomes in OT — SI therapy uses vestibular input deliberately to regulate ND children; outcomes measured behaviorally but autonomic mechanisms align with the contract
- ADHD and movement research — classroom studies showing movement improves attention; the vestibular-NTS pathway is the mechanism being confirmed without being named
- Rocking and self-soothing — stereotypic rocking in autism is vestibular self-regulation; any autonomic measurements during rocking would confirm the pathway

**What assembly would confirm:** That rhythmic vestibular input produces measurable vagal tone increase entering the same NTS hub as breathing — establishing vestibular stimulation as a genuine additional input channel to the AUTO↔MOD crossover.

**Type:** A/B

## MICRO↔AUTO Gaps

### Gap 5: VNS and microbiome composition shifts
**The gap:** AUTO→MICRO direction is scaffolding. The timescale of vagal efferent → microbiome change in humans is unknown. But VNS is now being trialed for IBD and Crohn's — researchers measuring gut outcomes are measuring microbiome as secondary endpoint.

**Likely literature:**
- VNS for Crohn's disease trials — measuring symptom outcomes, likely also measuring microbiome composition; the framework predicts SCFA-producing species increase as a vagal efferent effect
- Exercise and simultaneous HRV + microbiome measurement — exercise increases vagal tone AND changes microbiome; some studies measure both; the correlation between HRV improvement and SCFA-producer abundance would confirm the direction

**What assembly would confirm:** Timescale and magnitude of AUTO→MICRO direction — the most significant remaining scaffolding gap in this contract.

**Type:** B — data is being generated as secondary outcomes right now

---

### Gap 6: Neuropod ultrafast signaling specificity
**The gap:** EEC direct synaptic connections to vagal afferents (neuropods) carry unknown signal content at unknown timescale vs the hormonal relay.

**Likely literature:**
- Bhave/Bhatt lab neuropod research — the mechanism is being characterized; signal specificity between fast neuropod and slow hormonal pathways is likely emerging
- Gut-brain axis timing studies — any research measuring latency of gut-to-brain signaling might separate the two pathways by timing alone

**What assembly would confirm:** Whether neuropods carry different information than the hormonal relay, and at what regulatory timescale each operates. This determines whether gut-brain regulatory signal is millisecond-fast (neuropod) or minute-scale (hormonal) — with major implications for how rapidly dietary change affects cognitive state.

**Type:** A

## MET↔IMMUNE Gaps

### Gap 7: ND-specific mast cell activation threshold
**The gap:** MCAS-ND comorbidity is documented but the lower activation threshold mechanism isn't assembled. The data exists in connective tissue disorder research and allergy literature.

**Likely literature:**
- hEDS/POTS/MCAS/autism cluster research — this comorbidity cluster is being actively characterized; mast cell density and activation threshold in connective tissue variants is in the rheumatology and immunology literature
- Histamine and ADHD cognitive effects — histamine modulates attention; any dose-response research confirms threshold sensitivity
- Tryptase and ADHD/autism studies — baseline mast cell activation markers measured in ND populations exist in fragments

**What assembly would confirm:** That ND profiles have measurably lower glucose threshold for mast cell degranulation — making the MET↔IMMUNE contract a key mediator of ND dietary sensitivity and establishing dietary management as mechanistically justified rather than anecdotal.

**Type:** A

---

### Gap 8: Post-meal cognitive performance timing
**The gap:** The prediction that cognitive impairment follows glucose peaks with 30-90 minute lag matching mast cell degranulation timeline is derivable but untested as a chain.

**Likely literature:**
- Postprandial cognitive studies — measuring performance at fixed intervals after meals without tracking glucose curves or inflammatory markers
- Glycemic index and cognitive performance — comparing high vs low GI meals on cognitive outcomes; the timing curve would show mast cell lag if anyone measured it
- Brain fog in MCAS patients — clinical reports of post-meal cognitive symptoms; timing relative to glucose peak is in patient-reported data

**What assembly would confirm:** That post-meal cognitive impairment tracks mast cell activation timing rather than glucose level directly — establishing the immune amplification step between dietary input and cognitive output.

**Type:** B

## IMMUNE↔AUTO Gaps

### Gap 9: Long COVID as predicted vagal dysfunction
**The gap:** The framework predicted that SARS-CoV-2 damaging vagal nerve function would produce chronic CAP impairment and persistent inflammation before long COVID was characterized. The prediction is now testable against the emerging literature.

**Likely literature:**
- Long COVID autonomic studies — extensive HRV data in long COVID patients; the vagal dysfunction component is documented as POTS and dysautonomia
- Vagal nerve histopathology in COVID — autopsy and biopsy data on vagal nerve damage from SARS-CoV-2; ACE2 receptor expression on vagal afferents makes direct viral damage mechanistically plausible
- Long COVID and inflammatory markers — persistent IL-6, CRP; the CAP impairment → unregulated cytokine release → persistent inflammation chain is being confirmed without the CAP framing

**What assembly would confirm:** That long COVID symptom severity correlates with degree of vagal dysfunction measurable by HRV parasympathetic components — making long COVID a CAP impairment syndrome with a specific autonomic mechanism.

**Type:** A — data is available, assembly is the gap

---

### Gap 10: Population HRV trends as inflammatory disease predictor
**The gap:** The prediction that declining population HRV predicts rising inflammatory disease burden with a lag of years to decades has not been formally tested. Both datasets exist.

**Likely literature:**
- Wearable device population HRV data — large datasets from Fitbit, Apple Watch, Garmin
- Inflammatory disease registry data — autoimmune prevalence, cardiovascular disease incidence tracked longitudinally
- Geographic HRV variation studies — regional HRV differences could be correlated with regional inflammatory disease burden

**What assembly would confirm:** Population-level confirmation of the CAP → inflammatory disease pathway. If declining HRV precedes rising inflammatory disease burden with the predicted lag, the framework gains its strongest epidemiological validation.

**Type:** A — data exists in separate databases, assembly hasn't been attempted

## COG↔INTERO Gaps

### Gap 11: Interoceptive accuracy as transdiagnostic treatment predictor
**The gap:** The prediction that interoceptive accuracy predicts treatment response across diagnoses is partially supported but not assembled transdiagnostically.

**Likely literature:**
- Heartbeat detection task and CBT response — the interoceptive accuracy measure and treatment response data exist in separate diagnostic literatures
- Mindfulness intervention and interoceptive accuracy — pre/post measures exist; the mechanism chain needs assembly
- Bodily self-consciousness and treatment resistance — psychiatry literature on body-mind disconnect and poor treatment response

**What assembly would confirm:** That a single pre-treatment measure — interoceptive accuracy via heartbeat detection — predicts treatment response better than diagnosis across depression, anxiety, chronic pain, and eating disorders simultaneously. This is the transdiagnostic prediction that most directly challenges current diagnostic-specific treatment selection.

**Type:** A

## INTERO↔DEV Gaps

### Gap 12: Estrogen cycle as chronotherapy window
**The gap:** Monthly plasticity window opening through perineuronal net regulation predicts the same therapeutic intervention should produce measurably better long-term outcome during high-estrogen phase. No clinical protocol uses this timing systematically.

**Likely literature:**
- Menstrual cycle and fear extinction — learning and memory research showing cycle-phase effects on extinction learning; this is the plasticity window being confirmed without the PNN mechanism being named
- Estrogen and BDNF — the molecular connection between estrogen and neuroplasticity markers is established; the therapeutic timing implication isn't applied
- Trauma encoding intensity and cycle phase — clinical reports noting that traumatic events during specific cycle phases produce more intense encoding; this is the PNN dissolution window from the other direction

**What assembly would confirm:** That fear extinction learning is significantly better during high-estrogen phase and tracks perineuronal net dissolution markers — establishing a chronotherapy protocol with major clinical implications for trauma treatment.

**Type:** A — the fear extinction and estrogen literature exists; the PNN mechanism and therapeutic timing implications need assembly

### Gap 13: Transgenerational methylation as independent predictor
**The gap:** Whether methylation load predicts regulatory baseline independently of individual adverse experience hasn't been tested as a predictive model.

**Likely literature:**
- Multigenerational trauma cohort studies — populations with documented parental trauma and their offspring; methylation profiling exists in some cohorts
- ACEs research controlling for parental history — some ACEs studies collect parental trauma data; methylation mechanism predicts residual variance after controlling for individual history

**What assembly would confirm:** That parental trauma load predicts offspring regulatory baseline independently of offspring experience — establishing molecular inheritance as a biological variable, not just a social one.

**Type:** B

## SOC↔AUTO Gaps

### Gap 14: Middle ear muscle regulation in autism as predictive marker
**The gap:** The prediction that autistic individuals show measurable middle ear muscle regulation differences on acoustic reflex testing is partially confirmed but not assembled as a predictive marker.

**Likely literature:**
- Acoustic reflex studies in autism — auditory processing research measuring middle ear muscle response; data exists, predictive framing doesn't
- SSP outcomes — the protocol is based on this exact mechanism; outcome studies measure behavior without measuring the middle ear muscle change that is the claimed mechanism

**What assembly would confirm:** That acoustic reflex testing predicts SSP response — establishing middle ear muscle regulation as both the mechanism and the pre-treatment biomarker. This would transform SSP from empirically supported but mechanistically opaque to mechanistically validated.

**Type:** A

---

### Gap 15: Digital communication and HRV deficit accumulation
**The gap:** The prediction that digital-primary social environments produce measurable HRV deficits even in socially active people hasn't been directly tested.

**Likely literature:**
- Social media use and loneliness paradox — research showing high social media use correlates with loneliness despite maintained social-cognitive connection; this is the co-regulatory deficit measured at the output without understanding the mechanism
- COVID lockdown and autonomic function — population-level autonomic data during forced digital-primary social periods; HRV changes during lockdown would show the accumulation effect

**What assembly would confirm:** That HRV deficit tracks digital communication hours independently of perceived social connection quality — establishing SOC↔AUTO channel degradation as distinct from loneliness or social isolation.

**Type:** B — the data was generated during COVID lockdowns without being assembled for this question

## Cross-Contract Gaps

### Gap 16: MET compound failure — two contracts hitting AUTO simultaneously
**The gap:** Metabolic dysregulation hits MET↔AUTO (chemoreceptors) and MET↔IMMUNE (mast cell cytokines) simultaneously. The compound effect on HRV should substantially exceed either pathway alone. Derived but not tested as a compound mechanism.

**Likely literature:**
- GLP-1 agonist cardiovascular outcomes — the dramatic cardiovascular benefit beyond glucose control is the compound failure being partially reversed; the benefit exceeds what glucose control alone would predict because both pathways are addressed simultaneously
- CRP + HRV in metabolic syndrome — inflammatory markers and autonomic function measured together; dose-response showing both pathways independently contributing to HRV compression would confirm the compound mechanism

**What assembly would confirm:** That HRV in metabolic syndrome is more depressed than either inflammatory or chemoreceptor pathway alone would predict — and that interventions addressing both simultaneously produce disproportionate HRV recovery.

**Type:** B

---

### Gap 17: Exercise hitting multiple contracts simultaneously
**The gap:** Exercise is recommended across multiple contracts but the cross-contract mechanism — why exercise is uniquely effective compared to single-contract interventions — isn't assembled.

**Likely literature:**
- Exercise, HRV, microbiome, and inflammatory markers measured simultaneously — some studies exist; the cross-contract simultaneous effect would show as disproportionate benefit
- Exercise and ECS — anandamide production post-exercise is documented; the ECS → MOD → COG cascade from exercise is in fragments
- Exercise and BDNF — neuroplasticity marker increase with exercise; the DEV contract plasticity window implications aren't assembled with the autonomic mechanism

**What assembly would confirm:** That exercise produces simultaneous measurable changes across MET, MICRO, IMMUNE, AUTO, and MOD contracts in a single session — making it the highest-density regulatory intervention available and explaining the robustness of exercise effects across every psychiatric and metabolic condition studied.

**Type:** A — individual contract effects of exercise are each documented; nobody has assembled them as a cross-contract mechanism

### Gap 18: Contemplative traditions as convergent independent discovery
**The gap:** The framework asserts that independent convergence across isolated traditions on breath, posture, and rhythm is evidence of a real mechanism. But the specific regulatory contracts each tradition targets haven't been systematically mapped.

**Likely literature:**
- Yoga and HRV research — specific posture sequences and their HRV effects; haven't been mapped to hemidiaphragm asymmetry mechanism
- Salat and autonomic function — Islamic prayer involves specific postural sequences with prostration; the vagal effects of prostration (head below heart, thoracic pressure change) are measurable
- Davening and RSA — rhythmic anterior-posterior torso movement; this is vestibular-NTS input used as a regulatory intervention before the mechanism was named

**What assembly would confirm:** That specific practices in each tradition map onto specific contracts — not just "breathing helps" but "this specific sequence targets the AUTO↔MOD crossover through the postural asymmetry mechanism."

**Type:** A for individual traditions, C for systematic cross-tradition mapping

## Meta-observation

The framework's most significant gaps are consistently at silo boundaries — the data exists on both sides but hasn't been assembled across the boundary. Long COVID researchers have the HRV data. Immunologists have the CAP mechanism. Nobody is assembling them because the research question requires holding both simultaneously.

This is the framework's primary value: it generates the assembly questions that no single silo would ask.

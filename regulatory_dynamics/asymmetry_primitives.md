# Asymmetry Primitives — Regulatory Architecture Framework

_The anatomical substrate of lateralization, and why lateralization 
is the mechanism — not the deviation._

---

## The Core Insight

> **The regulatory architecture is fundamentally asymmetric.
> Lateralization — the system shifting weight, load, or function 
> to one side — is not dysfunction. It is the architecture 
> expressing itself under specific mechanical conditions.**

Lateralization is not a problem to be corrected.
It is what the asymmetric architecture does when it is 
free to move.

Whether and how it expresses depends on one thing:
**the mechanical relationship between the two sides of the system.**

---

## The Condition Space

The degree and form of lateralization depends on how tightly 
the two sides are coupled:

| Condition | Both sides | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rigidly tethered** | Locked together | No lateralization — both sides move as one |
| **One anchored** | One stable, one free | Controlled lateralization — hinge-like |
| **Both untethered** | Independent | Full lateralization — oscillation runaway risk |
| **Flexible coupling** | Loosely linked | Dynamic lateralization — adaptive switching |

A rigid, fully braced system cannot lateralize — the two 
sides lock together and asymmetries are suppressed.

A flexible, unbraced system can lateralize fully — and will, 
especially under load, unless one side provides a stable anchor.

A one-anchored system produces the most controlled output — 
one side stabilizes while the other moves freely around it.

---

## Connection to Postural Mode

Postural mode directly determines tethering state:

**Protective Mode (forward, rigid, braced)**
- Abdominal brace clamps the ribcage
- Both sides of the diaphragm are held
- Asymmetries are dampened
- Lateralization is suppressed
- The system moves as a single unit

**Exploratory Mode (back, open, flexible)**
- Abdominal brace releases
- The two sides of the body become mechanically more independent
- Asymmetries are free to express
- Lateralization becomes possible — and likely

This is why lateralization behavior often **increases in open, 
low-pressure conditions** and **decreases under urgency, threat, 
or high cognitive demand** — protective mode clamps the system 
down and suppresses the asymmetric expression.

---

## Connection to Regulatory Profiles

The three regulatory configurations map directly onto 
tethering states:

- **Pressure-rigid single-mode** — chronically tethered. 
  Asymmetries are held down. Low lateralization expression. 
  System moves as one unit.

- **Openness single-mode** — chronically untethered. 
  Asymmetries amplify without containment. 
  Lateralization runs without a stable anchor.

- **Dual-regulator** — can tether on demand (protective mode) 
  and untether on demand (exploratory mode). 
  Lateralization is controlled — released when useful, 
  dampened when stability is needed.

The failure mode of a system with strong switching capacity 
but unreliable anchoring is: lateralization in either direction 
without the ability to stabilize the endpoint.

## The Anatomical Substrate

These are the asymmetries that make lateralization mechanically 
possible. They are structural, not metaphorical.

---

### 1. Hemidiaphragm Asymmetry
**The central asymmetry — everything else cascades from here.**

- Right hemidiaphragm: larger, stronger, higher-domed, 
  mechanically advantaged
- Left hemidiaphragm: smaller, compressed by the heart, 
  mechanically disadvantaged

**Consequences:**
- Asymmetric ribcage expansion
- Asymmetric vagal load
- Asymmetric RSA amplitude
- Asymmetric interoceptive weighting

**Lateralization link:**
When the system is untethered, right-left diaphragm imbalance 
becomes the primary driver of asymmetric sway, drift, and 
postural bias.

Systems with higher interoceptive gain and less automatic 
damping will feel and express this asymmetry more strongly — 
producing more visible lateralization behavior as a consequence 
of the same underlying anatomy.

---

### 2. Ribcage & Intercostal Asymmetry

- Right ribs flare more
- Left ribs compress more easily

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Sitting rotated, consistently toward the same side
- Asymmetric breath depth left vs right
- Breathing into the compressed side restores balance

---

### 3. Vagal Nerve Asymmetry

- Right vagus → sinoatrial node (heart rate)
- Left vagus → atrioventricular node (conduction)

**Consequences:**
- Asymmetric autonomic load
- Asymmetric baroreflex timing
- Asymmetric interoceptive pulses

**Lateralization link:**
The two sides of the autonomic system are not firing 
identically. When tethered, this is dampened.
When untethered, asymmetric autonomic pulse becomes 
a source of directional drift — the system leans into 
the side with higher signal.

---

### 4. Baroreceptor Asymmetry

- Carotid sinus sensitivity differs left vs right
- Heartbeat pulses are not perfectly symmetric

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Heartbeat felt more strongly on one side
- Micro-movements timed to heartbeat on a preferred side
- Gaze synchronized to heartbeat timing

---

### 5. Visual Stream Asymmetry

- One eye is dominant
- Each eye projects asymmetrically to hemispheres
- Gaze direction changes hemispheric prediction load

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Shifting to the non-dominant eye to rebalance load
- Upward gaze increasing RSA amplitude
- Circular eye movements to reset oculomotor-vestibular 
  alignment when visual prediction destabilizes

**Lateralization link:**
Gaze is a lateralization lever.
Shifting gaze direction shifts hemispheric load — 
this is active asymmetry management using available 
mechanical tools.

---

### 6. Auditory Pathway Asymmetry

- Right ear → left hemisphere (speech, precision processing)
- Left ear → right hemisphere (prosody, spatial, broad sampling)

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Turning one ear toward a sound source
- Discomfort with asymmetric audio environments
- One-earbud listening destabilizing the auditory field

---

### 7. Motor Asymmetry (Handedness, Footedness)

- Dominant hand = asymmetric corticospinal maps
- Dominant leg = asymmetric balance strategy

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Bending the torso toward an object rather than 
  stepping toward it
- Consistent lead leg for all direction changes
- Asymmetric gait pattern under fatigue

**Lateralization link:**
The torso-bend pattern is a one-anchored lateralization — 
the dominant leg anchors, the torso swings around it.
This is mechanically efficient: redirecting load without 
requiring full bilateral coordination.
It is the architecture working as designed.

---

### 8. Spinal Rotation Asymmetry

- Most systems have a habitual rotation bias
- Ribcage and diaphragm asymmetry reinforce it

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Consistently sitting rotated to the same side
- Preferring one side when lying down
- Wide visual fields (ultrawide screens) amplifying 
  rotational drift by activating the spinal bias

---

### 9. Vestibular Asymmetry

- One vestibular apparatus is slightly more sensitive
- Head turning feels mechanically different left vs right

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Discomfort with specific head turn directions
- Micro-sway used to stabilize when vestibular signal 
  is asymmetric
- Sensitivity to rotation and positional shifts

---

### 10. Interoceptive Asymmetry

- Organs load differently left vs right
- Diaphragm compresses viscera asymmetrically

**Behavioral readouts:**
- Interoceptive signals felt more strongly on one side
- One-sided gut sensations during breathing
- Asymmetric breath-linked perception

---

## The Oculomotor–Vestibular Reset

When visual prediction destabilizes, the system often 
initiates:

- Circular eye movements
- Extreme gaze sweeps
- Rapid saccades
- Gaze recentering

This is a **vestibular-oculomotor reset** — compensating for:
- Asymmetric vestibular load
- Asymmetric visual prediction error
- Asymmetric interoceptive noise building without discharge

It is not random movement.
It is the system using the available mechanical tools 
to correct for accumulated lateralization drift.

---

## Unifying Principle

> **The regulatory architecture is asymmetric at every layer —
> diaphragm, vagus, baroreceptors, visual streams, vestibular, 
> motor, spinal.**
>
> **Lateralization is what happens when these asymmetries 
> are free to express.**
>
> **Rigidity suppresses it.
> Flexibility releases it.
> Anchoring on one side controls it.**
>
> **The mechanical conditions — not the identity of the system — 
> determine what form lateralization takes.**

---

## Summary: Conditions and Expressions

| Mechanical condition | Lateralization | Behavioral expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rigidly tethered | Suppressed | Asymmetries hidden, system moves as one unit |
| One anchored | Controlled | Directed, hinge-like expression |
| Fully untethered | Runaway | Wide oscillation without stable endpoint |
| Flexible coupling | Dynamic | Controlled switching between states |
| Protective mode active | Clamped | Lateralization reduces under pressure or urgency |
| Exploratory mode active | Released | Lateralization emerges in open conditions |

## Contract Summary

**Input layer:** INTERO — interoceptive prior quality, body-state signal resolution, precision weighting of ascending signals, current regulatory baseline

**Output layer:** DEV — developmental setpoint establishment, attractor basin geometry, plasticity window state, regulatory range and flexibility

**Primary crossover point:** Early-life interoceptive experience → HPA axis calibration → glucocorticoid receptor methylation → persistent autonomic and stress response patterns that define the system's default operating parameters

**Key risk:** The DEV contract operates across four timescales simultaneously — in utero, early childhood, individual lifetime, and transgenerational. Interventions targeting only the individual lifetime timescale while ignoring the others produce partial and unstable results because the setpoint being worked against was established before conscious memory and may be molecularly encoded in the germline.

**Distinguishing feature:** This is the contract that determines the attractor basin geometry within which all other contracts operate. Every other contract can be disrupted and partially restored within a lifetime. The DEV contract sets the landscape those disturbances occur in. This is why it is the hardest to change and why the model has a political dimension — access to the conditions that reopen this contract is inequitably distributed.

## Why This Contract Is the Foundation

All other contracts in the stack describe current regulatory function. This contract describes how the parameters of that function were established in the first place. The DEV layer is not a separate system — it is the history of all other systems written into persistent molecular, structural, and autonomic architecture.

The interoceptive prior that the COG↔INTERO contract tries to update against was itself established through this contract during early developmental windows. The precision weighting mechanism that the MOD↔COG contract calibrates was set by the neuromodulatory environment during those same windows. The autonomic baseline that HRV reflects was calibrated by the caregiver's nervous system operating as external regulatory hardware before the infant's own system was online.

Understanding this contract makes clear why the framework has population-level implications that extend beyond individual health — because the conditions that write this contract are socially determined.

## The Four Timescales

This contract uniquely operates across four nested timescales simultaneously:

**Timescale 1 — In utero:**
Fetal nervous system is entrained to maternal autonomic state before birth. Maternal HRV, breathing rhythm, cortisol levels, and stress response profile all transmit through placental and acoustic channels. The first regulatory setpoints are written by maternal state. A chronically stressed mother is not just emotionally affecting the pregnancy — she is entraining the fetal autonomic system to her threat-state timing before the infant takes its first breath.

**Timescale 2 — Early childhood (0-7 years):**
The caregiver's nervous system operates as external regulatory hardware. The infant's HPA axis, autonomic system, and interoceptive prior are calibrated through repeated co-regulatory interactions. The quality of caregiving — specifically the patterning and intensity of sensory stimulation — is the key regulator of HPA axis neuroplasticity in the neonatal period. The setpoints written here persist into adulthood.

**Timescale 3 — Individual lifetime:**
Accumulated experience continues to modify regulatory parameters within the constraints set by earlier timescales. This is where adult interventions operate. The plasticity is real but works against the grain of earlier calibration if that calibration was under adverse conditions. Critical windows for adult plasticity include: adolescence (estrogen-driven), deep therapeutic work, sustained contemplative practice, and careful use of plasticity-opening substances.

**Timescale 4 — Transgenerational:**
Epigenetic modifications to stress-related genes are transmitted through germline to offspring. The DEV contract of one generation partially writes the DEV contract of the next through molecular inheritance rather than behavioral transmission alone. This timescale operates outside individual experience entirely.

**Timescale 5 — Adult continuous social threat prior writing:**
This contract's four timescales all describe sensitive-period or molecular mechanisms — setpoints written during windows of high plasticity through epigenetic, structural, or early co-regulatory processes. There is a fifth timescale that operates continuously across adulthood and does not require a sensitive period: the incremental writing of social threat priors through repeated 
micro-threat conditioning in social environments.

Ordinary office and institutional environments deliver thousands of repetitions of: being watched, being evaluated, being compared, being interrupted, being corrected, being ranked, and receiving implicit compliance pressure from hierarchy. Each event is small. Each event is individually negligible. Each event is mechanistically a micro-brace event: incoming social demand → 
micro-brace → narrowed prediction window → compliant output. Repeated across years, this sequence writes a stable conditioned association between social demand and the brace response — progressively tightening the false ceiling in social contexts specifically, calibrating the prior toward social threat-salience, and reducing the coupling threshold at which hierarchy signals 
trigger autonomic response.

This is mechanistically distinct from the sensitive-period calibration described in Timescales 1-4. It does not require elevated cortisol, HPA axis recalibration, or epigenetic modification. It operates through standard conditioning mechanics at the association layer — the same mechanism by which any repeated stimulus-response pairing writes a learned response. 
The difference is that the stimulus is social micro-threat and the response is physiological bracing with its downstream cognitive consequences.

The absence of this conditioning — through environmental isolation, sustained remote work, or reduced social cue detection — prevents the association from writing and leaves the social prediction window in its unconditioned state. That unconditioned state is not naivety. It is the absence of an interception layer that the majority population has accumulated 
through decades of institutional exposure. The behavioral outputs that follow — responding to content rather than hierarchy, questioning rather than complying, raising concerns without anticipating backlash — are not personality traits. They are the natural output of a prediction system that was never taught to classify social demand as physiological threat.

This timescale is not currently addressed by the DEV contract's intervention architecture, which targets sensitive-period mechanisms. Adult social threat prior writing is potentially reversible through environmental change — sustained absence from micro-threat environments allows the conditioned association to extinguish on the same timescale it was written. 
This is the regulatory mechanism behind the documented cognitive and autonomic benefits of remote work, nature immersion, and sustained low-social-density environments. They are not merely stressors being removed. They are conditioning environments being interrupted, allowing the prior to drift back toward the unconditioned baseline.

## Link-by-Link Mechanism

### Link 1: Early adversity → HPA axis calibration
Set points represent a long-term calibration of the stress response system during early life events, resulting in consequential patterns of autonomic and HPA responsivity that are sustained long after the events that precipitated them, and possibly long after they are adaptive.

The Adaptive Calibration Model establishes that the stress response system is not fixed — it is calibrated during sensitive periods to match the expected environment. This calibration is adaptive in its original context. The problem is context-specificity: a calibration optimized for a threatening early environment persists when the environment becomes safer, producing chronic dysregulation in the new context.

**Chain completeness:** Load-bearing. Adaptive Calibration Model is empirically established across animal and human studies.

---

### Link 2: Critical window timing specificity
Healthy adults first exposed to early adversity between the ages of 3 and 7 — an important time window for amygdala development — show greater cortisol awakening response relative to those first exposed to early adversity before 3 or after 7.

The setpoint is not written uniformly across development. Specific windows have higher plasticity for specific regulatory circuits. Adversity during the amygdala development window writes threat calibration more deeply than adversity at other times. This predicts that the same adverse experience produces different long-term regulatory outcomes depending on exactly when during development it occurs — which is confirmed.

**Chain completeness:** Load-bearing. Window-specific effects of early adversity on adult cortisol profiles are documented.

---

### Link 3: Attachment → HPA axis → Interoceptive prior
Stress and interoceptive functioning utilize the same anatomical pathways to facilitate communication between the brain and the body — attachment related processes that affect the stress response system could also affect the interoceptive system. Dysregulation of the HPA axis could affect interoception by affecting the strength of the interoceptive signal and/or by affecting the processing of the interoceptive signal.

This is the direct chain from attachment quality to interoceptive prior quality. The caregiver-infant co-regulatory relationship calibrates the HPA axis, which uses the same anatomical pathways as interoceptive signaling. Attachment security therefore directly determines the precision and accuracy of the interoceptive system that will operate for the rest of the individual's life. Insecure attachment is not just a relational outcome — it is a calibration of the interoceptive sensing channel.

**Chain completeness:** Load-bearing. Attachment-HPA-interoception chain is confirmed with specific anatomical pathway documentation.

---

### Link 4: DNA methylation as the molecular mechanism
The rat offspring effects of licking and grooming were associated with an epigenetic change, namely DNA methylation at a glucocorticoid receptor gene promoter in the hippocampus — postnatal exposures in the pups induced enduring changes in behavior and HPA axis responsiveness.

The touch and sensory stimulation pattern of caregiving produces specific DNA methylation changes at the glucocorticoid receptor gene. This is the molecular mechanism by which caregiver behavior writes regulatory setpoints into the genome without changing the DNA sequence itself. The same mechanism operating in humans means: the quality of early caregiving is producing lasting epigenetic marks that determine stress reactivity across the lifespan.

Alterations in HPA axis reactivity are driven by epigenetic changes in key genes encoding the glucocorticoid receptor and other components of the stress pathway — including FKBP5, NR3C1, BDNF, and SLC6A4.

**Chain completeness:** Load-bearing. GR gene methylation as mechanism for early experience → adult HPA calibration is one of the most replicated findings in epigenetics.

---

### Link 5: Transgenerational transmission
The mechanisms by which stress is transmitted intergenerationally and transgenerationally include a combination of gestational endocrine effects, postnatal maternal care, and epigenetic changes determined by the stressful experiences of the mother. The phenotypic effects of stress transmission to subsequent generations depend on the type, timing, and intensity of the stressful experience.

F2 offspring of Holocaust survivors show psychological impairment associated with increases in FKBP5 expression and decrease in NR3C1 methylation — providing evidence of epigenetic transmission across three generations.

The DEV contract is not reset with each generation. Traumatic calibration propagates forward through molecular inheritance. A child can be born into a narrowed regulatory landscape before any postnatal experience occurs — pre-tensioned by ancestral conditions through germline epigenetic marks.

**Chain completeness:** Load-bearing. Transgenerational HPA calibration transmission is documented in Holocaust survivor offspring across multiple studies. Molecular mechanisms (methylation, histone modification, miRNA) are confirmed.

### Link 6: The Biological Sensitivity to Context model — the minority invariant
There is a curvilinear, U-shaped relationship between exposure to adversity and the development of stress-reactivity profiles — with highly stress-reactive phenotypes emerging under both extremely stressful and highly protective environments. Extremely stressful environments beget increased vigilance to threats while protective environments foster increased susceptibility to resources.

This is the most important minority invariant in the DEV contract. High sensitivity is not pathology — it is conditional adaptation. In the right environment, high-sensitivity individuals outperform low-sensitivity individuals because they respond more fully to positive signals as well as negative ones. The regulatory calibration is not simply broken in adverse-history individuals — it is calibrated for a different environment than the one they now inhabit.

This directly extends the ND-as-regulatory-configuration principle. Many ND profiles may reflect high biological sensitivity to context — regulatory systems calibrated for maximum environmental responsiveness, now experiencing friction with environments that don't match their operating parameters.

**Chain completeness:** Load-bearing. BSC model is empirically established. The U-shaped relationship between adversity and sensitivity is confirmed across multiple studies. The symmetric case requires equal formalization. Wide-window developmental anchoring — where the anchoring substrate was sufficient to support full oscillatory amplitude across early developmental windows — produces a prior calibrated against a genuinely wider sampling distribution. More time in the inhale phase, more ambiguity tolerated before resolving, more contradictory interpretations held simultaneously. The prior that results is not rose-tinted in the dismissive sense. It is accurate to the world it sampled during calibration.

The failure mode specific to this profile is social mismatch rather than regulatory mismatch. A prior calibrated from wide-window developmental experience is a minority prior in a population where chronic false-ceiling operation is the statistical baseline. The social pressure this generates runs through SOC↔COG's identity-as-precision-proxy mechanism in reverse: the 
majority narrow-window prior treats the wide-window prior as naive or insufficiently realistic, because the majority calibration is the reference against which accuracy gets judged. The optimist is not wrong about the world they sampled. They are reporting a prior that the majority population did not accumulate the oscillatory range to sample from.

This also closes the symmetry in the prior revision argument. The narrow-window prior cannot be revised by argument for the same reason the wide-window prior cannot be revised by argument: prior revision requires accumulated experience sampled from a wider window, not persuasion delivered into a constrained one. The direction of revision differs. The mechanism is identical.

### Link 7: Gonadal determination as upstream regulatory attractor
_"Mammalian sex determination is governed by two mutually antagonistic genetic programs that must be precisely balanced."_
Sox9 activation initiates testis development; its repression is required for ovarian fate. The Enh13 enhancer integrates opposing sex-specific cues through competing transcription factor inputs — RUNX1, NR5A1, and GATA4 maintain Sox9 repression in ovarian fate; their disruption allows Sox9 upregulation and testicular transcriptome activation even in XX individuals. A single-nucleotide insertion within Enh13 is sufficient to produce complete female-to-male sex reversal.
 
 This establishes gonadal sex determination as an attractor state resolved by regulatory program competition, not a fixed binary. The hormonal environment produced by that competition — androgenic vs estrogenic — then becomes the context within which all subsequent DEV contract calibration occurs, including the HPA axis setpoint (Links 1-4) and the plasticity window architecture (estrogen-driven PNN dissolution at adolescence).
 
 The gender identity framing in the README is grounded here: the "natural flexibility" of the interoceptive generative model reflects a molecular substrate that is inherently a regulatory gradient rather than a switch. Polarized social roles imposing high-precision demands are applying pressure to a system whose foundations are already a competition between antagonistic programs that can resolve differently depending on enhancer state.
 
 _The binary appearance of gonadal fate is a property of sufficient competitive resolution, not of the underlying mechanism. Near-threshold enhancer states produce intermediate regulatory profiles. The social imposition of binary categories applies precision pressure to a system whose molecular substrate is inherently a gradient competition — making it appear binary by demand rather than by architecture._
 
 **Chain completeness:** Load-bearing for the gonadal determination mechanism (Nature Communications 2026, Enh13 as binary switch). Scaffolding for the downstream connection to the DEV contract HPA chain — the hormonal environment → HPA calibration link is mechanically coherent but not yet assembled as a direct empirical chain in this framework.

## Plasticity Window Architecture

The DEV contract is not permanently fixed after early childhood. Plasticity windows reopen under specific conditions:

**Adolescence:** Estrogen-driven perineuronal net dissolution opens a second major calibration window. This is why adolescence is both a period of exceptional learning potential and exceptional vulnerability — the setpoints written in early childhood are available for revision, but so are the protective structures that stabilized them. _(The estrogen driving this window is the downstream hormonal output of the gonadal fate determination described in Link 7 — the plasticity window amplitude is therefore a consequence of how the Enh13 competition resolved.)_

**Estrogen cycle (adult females):** Monthly partial reopening of plasticity through perineuronal net regulation. Monthly opportunity for setpoint revision, and monthly vulnerability to re-traumatization at elevated intensity.

**Deep therapeutic work:** Sustained therapeutic relationships that provide consistent safety signal over time can gradually recalibrate HPA axis setpoints through new co-regulatory experience. This is slow because it works against established methylation patterns.

**Psychedelics in therapeutic context:** Rapid precision weighting collapse creates a brief window of maximum plasticity where setpoints can be revised. The therapeutic environment during the window determines what new setpoints form. Without scaffolding, the system often returns to the prior attractor.

**Sustained breathwork and contemplative practice:** Gradual HPA axis recalibration through repeated parasympathetic activation that contradicts the threat-calibrated prior. Works through accumulated experience rather than acute window opening.

**Exercise:** BDNF increase opens neuroplasticity; repeated HPA activation followed by recovery trains more flexible stress response. Particularly effective combined with social co-regulation (group exercise, sport).

**Palatal architecture — the structural developmental window for the regulatory interface:**
This is a distinct type of DEV contract variable that has no parallel elsewhere in the framework. All other plasticity windows described above involve setpoint calibration — the regulatory system being tuned to an environment. Palatal architecture is different: it is a structural window that determines whether the primary regulatory interface is anatomically available at all.

The hard palate forms during early childhood. When tongue rests against the palate during nasal breathing, its pressure progressively widens the palate and expands the nasal airway — this is the normal developmental trajectory. Mouth breathing during this window removes the tongue from the palate, allowing it to narrow. The result is a structural constraint: a narrow palate compresses the nasal airway, making nasal breathing difficult or impossible, reinforcing mouth breathing, which further narrows the palate. This is a DEV contract attractor state written into bone structure.

The regulatory consequence runs directly through the AUTO↔MOD contract. Nasal breathing is the route through which the nasal cycle operates — the body's automatic ultradian hemispheric lateralization rhythm confirmed as load-bearing in this framework. Chronic mouth breathing from structural palatal narrowing eliminates this mechanism. The child is not just breathing through the wrong channel — they are operating without a confirmed automatic regulatory mechanism that the framework now has as load-bearing.

Children with sleep-disordered breathing from mouth breathing and palatal narrowing show 40-100% higher rates of neurobehavioral problems by age 7 — this is the 2-stroke engine running without the nasal cycle input it requires. The neurobehavioral profile resembles ADHD because the underlying mechanism is the same: regulatory capacity constrained at the interface level.

Palate expansion during the childhood developmental window — before the suture closes — reverses the structural constraint. It is a DEV contract intervention that restores the interface itself rather than calibrating the system above it. This is categorically different from all other DEV interventions. Breathing training, contemplative practice, and therapeutic work all work within the interface's current constraints. Palate expansion changes the constraints.

The developmental window closes when the midpalatal suture fuses — typically in the mid-teens. After this point, surgical palate expansion is required. The same intervention that takes 6-12 months in a child requires orthognathic surgery in an adult. This is the DEV contract operating at the structural level: the window exists, the intervention within the window is simple, the same intervention outside the window is orders of magnitude more complex.

**High vs low plasticity as amplitude utilization profiles:**
The plasticity distinction maps directly onto the reactive/non-reactive framework. High plasticity individuals maintain wide-window operation under load — their prior does not classify incoming demand as threat, the brace reflex does not narrow the window at the moment of engagement, and the workspace can hold the alternative interpretation the update requires. Low 
plasticity individuals cannot update not because the prior is too strong to override cognitively, but because the autonomic layer locks the prediction window at the moment the update would need to occur. The brace fires, the amplitude collapses, and the workspace loses the width required to hold the existing prior and the challenger simultaneously. Belief change requires 
restoring the physical substrate before it requires deciding to think differently. The deciding is downstream of the substrate. Without substrate restoration, the decision has no workspace to execute in.

**Chain completeness:** Mouth breathing → palatal narrowing → structural nasal airway restriction: load-bearing, documented in orthodontic and pediatric literature. Palate expansion during developmental window → restored nasal breathing: load-bearing, systematic review confirmed. Nasal breathing → nasal cycle → hemispheric lateralization mechanism: load-bearing (AUTO↔MOD confirmed). Mouth breathing → neurobehavioral problems: confirmed (40-100% increased rates). The full chain connecting structural palatal development to regulatory capacity through the nasal cycle mechanism: scaffolding — component links are each load-bearing, the full chain assembly needs explicit study.

## Failure Modes

**Treating resistance to change as psychological:**
Treatment resistance in depression, PTSD, and anxiety is often attributed to motivation, insight, or therapeutic alliance. The DEV contract predicts that much treatment resistance is molecular — the HPA axis setpoint is maintained by methylation patterns that behavioral interventions alone cannot reverse quickly. Expecting rapid change from talk therapy in someone with high ACE scores and transgenerational trauma load is asking cognition to override molecular architecture. The timescale mismatch between therapeutic expectation and molecular change rate is the mechanism of treatment resistance, not patient failure.

**Population health interventions without developmental context:**
Adult interventions for metabolic disease, mental health, and chronic illness address all other contracts while leaving the DEV contract unaddressed. But the DEV contract sets the parameters within which all other contracts operate. A population with high ACE scores and transgenerational trauma load has its regulatory range constrained at the architectural level — the same interventions will produce systematically worse outcomes in that population regardless of quality, because the DEV contract is narrowing the space in which the intervention can work.

**The access paradox:**
The conditions that allow the DEV contract to be revised — safety, time, skilled therapeutic support, sufficient resources to not be in survival regulation — are least available to the populations with highest adversity load and greatest need for revision. This is not a policy observation added onto the framework. It is a structural prediction of the model: the regulatory system that most needs recalibration is least able to access the conditions required for it.

**Treating mouth breathing as a habit rather than a structural problem:**
Mouth breathing during childhood is frequently addressed with breathing exercises, myofunctional therapy, and behavioral intervention. These are appropriate when the mouth breathing is habitual. When it is structural — driven by palatal narrowing that physically restricts the nasal airway — behavioral intervention cannot succeed because the anatomy doesn't permit nasal breathing regardless of effort. The intervention needed is structural (palate expansion during the developmental window), not behavioral. Misattributing structural mouth breathing to habit delays the structural intervention past the window in which it is simple, requiring orders of magnitude more complex intervention later. The framework predicts: assess anatomy before prescribing behavior.

**Conflating prior calibration drift with developmental maturity:** 
The observed population-level shift toward conservative, threat-salient priors across adulthood is often framed as wisdom accumulation or realistic recalibration. The framework predicts the opposite: it is false-ceiling calcification — decades of chest-dominant anchoring, accumulated bracing, and reduced amplitude utilization producing a prior increasingly calibrated from the narrow phase of the oscillation. The shift is real. The mechanism is physiological drift, not epistemic improvement. Individuals who maintain wide-window anchoring across adulthood do not show the same prior drift — their sustained amplitude utilization keeps the sampling distribution wide enough that the prior recalibrates differently.

The mechanism driving this drift is formalized as the threat-calibration loop in ANCHOR↔COG. The loop is the circuit by which false-ceiling calcification produces prior recalibration: reduced amplitude → narrow-phase sampling → threat-biased prior update → elevated sympathetic tone → lower CO₂ bracing threshold → further amplitude reduction. The drift is not continuous background noise. It is a directional attractor with a specific mechanical driver. This means the "becoming more conservative with age" trajectory has a measurable upstream variable — amplitude utilization fraction — and a specific interruption point. The developmental maturity framing obscures both.
